I'll never forget the first time I sprayed La Petite Robe Noire Intense on my wrist at the Guerlain counter. It was December, snowing outside, and I'd just come from a disappointing date where the guy spent forty minutes explaining cryptocurrency. I needed something to reset my senses, something indulgent. The sales associate handed me the bottle—that gorgeous black dress silhouette—and said, "This one's naughtier than the original."

She wasn't kidding.

## The Cherry Bomb That Changed Everything

### First Spray: Amaretto at Midnight

Right out of the gate, you're hit with cherry. Not maraschino cherry from a sad sundae. Not even fresh cherry from a farmer's market. This is cherry liqueur that's been aged in oak barrels and served in a dimly lit bar where everyone's wearing velvet. The almond note wraps around it like they're dancing together, creating this boozy, marzipan-adjacent opening that's either going to make you swoon or run for the hills.

There's a reason this is called "Intense." The original La Petite Robe Noire was playful, a bit coquettish with its fruity-floral bounce. This flanker? She grew up, got a job at an art gallery, and started wearing darker lipstick. The bergamot tries to brighten things up in those first five minutes, but it's fighting a losing battle against that cherry-almond bomb.

### That Licorice Twist Nobody Asked For (But Secretly Needed)

Here's where it gets interesting, and honestly, a bit divisive. About twenty minutes in, this licorice note emerges from the heart. Now, I know what you're thinking—licorice in perfume is risky business. It can veer into cough syrup territory faster than you can say "Sambuca." But Guerlain, being Guerlain, somehow makes it work.

The licorice doesn't dominate. Instead, it adds this dark, slightly anisic depth that prevents the cherry from becoming a one-note wonder. The rose (specifically Taif rose, which has this honeyed, spicy quality) softens the edges, creating a contrast that feels almost baroque in its complexity. It's like someone draped a silk scarf over a leather jacket.

## Why This Isn't Your Safe Blind-Buy

### The Sweetness Factor That Divides Nations

Let me be brutally honest: this perfume is SWEET. Not Britney Spears Fantasy sweet, but definitely more than your average adult-oriented fragrance. The tonka bean and vanilla in the base are generous—some might say too generous. I've worn this on days when I thought I could handle it, only to find myself overwhelmed by hour six, wondering if I'd become a walking crème brûlée.

If you're someone who recoils at the thought of gourmand fragrances, who prefers their perfumes bone-dry and austere, La Petite Robe Noire Intense will be your nightmare. It's unapologetically dessert-like, and while I appreciate that about it (most days), it's not going to win over the chypre purists.

### Sillage That Announces Your Arrival

Strong sillage isn't always a blessing. I wore this to a small dinner party once—just eight people around a table—and by the time we got to dessert, my friend leaned over and whispered, "Sophie, I can taste your perfume in my tiramisu." Not exactly the compliment I was hoping for.

This is a fragrance that projects. Hard. The first three hours, you're leaving a trail that could guide ships to harbor. Which is fantastic if you're going to a crowded bar or a winter evening event where you want to be noticed. Less fantastic if you're in close quarters or have colleagues who are sensitive to scent.

## The Dry Down Where Everything Makes Sense

### Patchouli's Redemption Arc

I have a complicated relationship with patchouli. Too much of it, and I'm transported back to that yoga studio in 2009 where everyone smelled like they'd been marinating in essential oils. But here, in the base of La Petite Robe Noire Intense, patchouli plays the grounding role it was born to play.

Around hour four or five, when the cherry has finally calmed down and the licorice has mostly evaporated, the patchouli emerges with this earthy, slightly woody warmth that balances all that sweetness. It's like the fragrance finally exhales. The vanilla is still present—this is Guerlain, they're not going to abandon their signature—but it's creamier now, less sugar-bomb and more sophisticated custard.

This is the stage where I actually fall in love with the perfume. The opening is exciting but exhausting. The dry down? That's where you want to live.

### Longevity That Laughs at Other Fragrances

One area where La Petite Robe Noire Intense absolutely excels: staying power. I'm talking 8-10 hours easily, and I've had it linger on clothes for days. There's something about that oriental-vanilla base that just clings to fabric like it's signed a lease.

For the price point (hovering around $100-$130 depending on where you shop and what size you get), that longevity matters. You're not going to need to reapply this at lunch. One morning spray will carry you through dinner, drinks, and probably halfway through the next day if you're wearing a sweater.

## When You Should (and Shouldn't) Reach for This Bottle

### The Perfect Storm of Circumstances

This perfume has a very specific sweet spot—pun intended. It's made for cold weather evenings when you want to feel dramatic. Think: dinner dates in dimly lit restaurants, theatre openings, holiday parties where someone's going to spike the eggnog. I wore it to a New Year's Eve party in a velvet dress and felt like I'd finally understood the assignment.

It's also surprisingly good for romantic occasions, assuming your partner enjoys sweet scents. There's something undeniably sensual about that cherry-almond-vanilla combination when it's worn in the right context. My partner once described it as "what I imagine fancy hotels in Vienna smell like," which might be the most oddly specific compliment this perfume has ever received.

### Where It Falls Flat on Its Face

Do not—and I cannot stress this enough—wear this to the office. Unless you work somewhere incredibly casual or entirely alone, this is too much for professional settings. It's also terrible in heat. I made the mistake of wearing it in late spring once, and it turned into this cloying, suffocating cloud that followed me around like a sugar-coated ghost.

It's also not great for minimalists. If your fragrance collection consists of clean musks and subtle citruses, this is going to feel like someone's screaming in your face. It demands attention, and not everyone wants their perfume to be the loudest thing in the room.

## How It Stacks Up Against the Competition

### The Original LPRN: A Tale of Two Sisters

Comparing this to the original La Petite Robe Noire is inevitable. The original is brighter, more overtly fruity with its berry cocktail opening, and generally more wearable for daytime. It's the sister who shows up on time and brings flowers.

La Petite Robe Noire Intense is the sister who arrives late, slightly tipsy, with better stories. She's more polarizing but also more memorable. If you own the original and love it, you might find Intense too heavy. But if you found the original a bit too sweet and simple, Intense offers more complexity—even if that complexity comes wrapped in a cherry-vanilla bow.

### The Viktor & Rolf Bonbon Comparison

People inevitably compare this to Viktor & Rolf's Bonbon, and I get it—both are unapologetically gourmand, both feature prominent caramel-adjacent notes. But Bonbon is sweeter, more candy-like, whereas La Petite Robe Noire Intense has that rose and patchouli backbone that makes it feel more grown-up.

Bonbon is what you wear when you want to smell edible. LPRN Intense is what you wear when you want to smell like you've just returned from somewhere fabulous and slightly decadent. Different moods entirely.

## The Verdict from Someone Who's Worn This for Three Years

Here's the thing: I don't reach for La Petite Robe Noire Intense often, but when I do, nothing else will scratch that particular itch. It's a mood perfume. A special occasion player. And I'm okay with that.

Is it worth the money? If you love sweet, oriental fragrances and don't mind making an entrance, absolutely. The longevity and projection alone justify the price. But if you're expecting versatility or subtlety, keep walking. This perfume doesn't do subtle. It doesn't want to.

Would I recommend it as a blind buy? No. This absolutely requires testing first. Go to a Guerlain counter, spray it, live with it for a few hours. See if that cherry-almond opening charms you or overwhelms you. Notice whether the sweetness feels indulgent or cloying. Only you can make that call.

But for those cold winter nights when you want to feel like a character in a French film, when you want your perfume to be as memorable as your outfit, when subtlety can take a backseat to sheer sensory pleasure—La Petite Robe Noire Intense delivers. Just maybe don't wear it to your next video conference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is La Petite Robe Noire Intense different from the original La Petite Robe Noire?

The Intense version is significantly heavier, sweeter, and more oriental-leaning than the original. While the original LPRN features a brighter, fruitier profile with cherry and berries, the Intense amplifies the cherry note, adds prominent almond and licorice, and features a much richer vanilla-tonka base. Think of it as the evening wear version while the original is more day-to-night versatile.

### Does La Petite Robe Noire Intense smell like cherries the whole time?

Not entirely. The cherry is very dominant for the first hour or two, but as it develops, the rose, licorice, and eventually the vanilla-patchouli base take over. By hour four, it's more of a warm, sweet oriental with just a whisper of cherry in the background. The evolution is actually one of its best features—if you can make it past that intense opening.

### Is this perfume too sweet for someone who doesn't usually like gourmands?

Probably, yes. This is firmly in gourmand territory with its cherry-almond-vanilla composition. If you typically prefer fresh, green, or woody fragrances, La Petite Robe Noire Intense will likely feel too heavy and dessert-like. However, the licorice and patchouli do provide some depth that makes it more sophisticated than your average candy-sweet gourmand. Sample it first if you're on the fence.

### What's the best time of year to wear La Petite Robe Noire Intense?

Fall and winter, hands down. This is a cold-weather perfume through and through. The heavy sweetness and strong projection become overwhelming in heat and humidity. I'd say October through March is its ideal window, with December and January being absolute peak season. Wear it when you need a sweater and it'll sing.

### How many sprays of La Petite Robe Noire Intense should I use?

Start with one or two sprays, maximum. This is a strong fragrance with serious projection. I typically do one spray on my collarbone or one on the inside of my coat. More than three sprays and you risk becoming that person everyone can smell from across the room. The longevity is excellent, so you don't need much to make an impact that lasts all evening.
