My mother wore Eternity to my cousin's wedding in 1993. I was seven, and I remember thinking she smelled like what princesses probably smelled like—clean, beautiful, untouchable in the best way. Fast forward thirty-odd years, and I'm standing in a department store, testing it again, and that same feeling washes over me. Except now I'm old enough to understand what I'm smelling, to pick apart the lily from the freesia, to notice how it sits differently on my skin than it did on hers.

Eternity launched in 1988, and honestly? It smells like it. But that's not necessarily a criticism. This is the perfume equivalent of a perfectly tailored white blazer—yes, it's classic, yes, it references a specific era, but if you know how to wear it, it still works.

## The Opening: Brighter Than You Remember

### That First Spray Surprise

I'm not going to lie—I expected Eternity to smell dated the moment it hit my skin. The bottle alone screams late '80s minimalism with that heavy glass and the name etched in all caps. But the opening surprised me. It's bright. Almost citrusy-green with mandarin and a sage note that feels more modern than you'd think. There's this crisp quality, like biting into a barely-ripe pear while standing in a florist shop.

The freesia comes through immediately too, and this is where Eternity shows its age a bit. Freesia was THE note of the late '80s and early '90s—every perfume from that era has it. But here, it works because it's not trying to be trendy. It's just... there, doing its job, creating this airy, slightly soapy opening that feels scrubbed-clean and optimistic.

### The Herbal Whisper Nobody Talks About

That sage note deserves more attention. It's subtle, almost buried under the florals, but it adds this unexpected herbal quality that keeps Eternity from being just another white floral bomb. It reminds me a bit of what rosemary does in Le Labo's perfumes—it grounds everything, makes it feel less perfume-y and more like something that could exist in nature (even though this absolutely could not exist in nature).

## The Heart: Where Eternity Becomes Itself

### Lily's Starring Role

Give it twenty minutes. That's when Eternity stops being polite and becomes what it truly is: a lily showcase. Not the heavy, funeral lily you might be dreading, but lily with manners. Lily that went to finishing school. It's creamy and white and utterly unapologetic, supported by marigold and narcissus that add this almost honeyed sweetness.

I wore this for a week straight to really understand it, and the lily heart is where opinions will divide. If you're someone who finds white florals too much—too bridal, too formal, too mother-of-the-bride—then Eternity probably isn't your perfume. But if you appreciate white florals when they're done with restraint and elegance, this is textbook perfume construction.

### The Marigold Mystery

Marigold is listed in the notes, and I swear I can smell it, but it's weird because marigold usually reads spicy or even slightly bitter. Here, it just adds warmth. It's like the difference between standing in sunlight versus standing in shade—same temperature, different feeling. This is what makes Eternity wearable for everyday instead of just special occasions. That subtle warmth prevents it from being too cool, too untouchable.

### How It Compares to the Competition

Let's talk about Estée Lauder Beautiful for a second, because if you're considering Eternity, you've probably smelled Beautiful. They're sisters from the same era, both white floral powerhouses. Beautiful is richer, more rose-forward, more "I'm going to the opera." Eternity is cleaner, more lily-centric, more "I'm getting married in a garden." Beautiful has better longevity on me (we'll get to that), but Eternity is easier to wear casually. I'd reach for Beautiful for a black-tie event. Eternity for a summer lunch date.

## The Dry Down and Reality Check

### Where It Goes Soft

After about four hours, Eternity settles into this sandalwood-amber base that's pleasant but—and here's my first real criticism—kind of generic. The opening and heart are distinctive enough, but the dry down could be almost any clean white musk from that era. It's pretty. It's comfortable. It's also a bit forgettable.

The musk here is very '80s in its cleanliness. It's that "fresh laundry" smell that everyone wanted back then, before we all decided we wanted to smell like oud and tobacco instead. I don't hate it, but I don't love it either. It just exists, doing its job, letting the perfume fade gracefully without drama.

### The Longevity Issue

Here's my second criticism: six to eight hours sounds good on paper, but the reality is that Eternity becomes a skin scent after about three to four hours. The sillage is moderate at best—your coworkers won't smell you from across the room, which might be exactly what you want. But if you're hoping for that "smelled you before I saw you" effect, this won't deliver.

I've found that layering helps. I'll use an unscented lotion as a base, or even layer it over a similar white floral body wash. That extends the longevity to a solid workday, but straight from the bottle? It's intimate rather than projecting.

## Who Should (and Shouldn't) Wear This

### The Ideal Eternity Wearer

This is for you if you're tired of every perfume smelling like either fruit salad or a spice market. If you appreciate restraint. If you want something recognizable without being basic. I've been wearing it to the office, and the responses have been interesting—older colleagues compliment it with nostalgia, younger ones ask what it is because they've never smelled anything like it.

It's genuinely lovely for brides or wedding guests. That whole "something borrowed" thing? Borrow this from your mom or aunt who probably has a bottle from 1992 that still smells perfect (perfume doesn't really expire despite what anyone tells you).

### Who Should Run Away

If you're deep into niche perfumes and expect weirdness or complexity, Eternity will bore you to tears. This is not cerebral perfume. It's not trying to challenge you or make you think. It's pretty, it's polite, it's classic. That's its job.

Also, if you're under 25 and trying to establish your signature scent, this might make you smell like you're wearing your mother's perfume. Because you basically are. That's not necessarily bad, but be aware of what you're signaling.

## The Value Proposition

### Price Reality

Eternity typically runs between $60-$90 for a 100ml bottle, which in today's perfume market is actually reasonable. You're not getting niche-level complexity, but you're getting competent, wearable elegance from a house that knows what it's doing.

Compare that to newer white florals like Jo Malone's White Jasmine & Mint (which I actually prefer for modern white floral needs) at nearly double the price, and Eternity starts looking like a smart buy. Though honestly, if you have the budget, the Jo Malone is more versatile and interesting.

### The Reformulation Question

Let's address the elephant in the room: has Eternity been reformulated? Almost certainly. Every perfume from the '80s has been. But having compared my mother's ancient bottle with a current one, the differences are subtle. The current version might be slightly weaker, slightly cleaner, but the DNA is intact. Unlike some classics that have been butchered (I'm looking at you, Dior Dune), Eternity has maintained its core identity.

## When and Where to Wear It

### Seasonal Versatility

Spring and summer are obvious choices—that freesia-lily combo feels naturally aligned with warmer weather. But I've been wearing it in early fall, and it works surprisingly well. The amber base gives it just enough warmth to not feel jarringly out of season when temperatures drop.

Winter though? That's pushing it. This is too delicate for deep winter. Save it for when you can see spring approaching.

### Specific Scenarios Where It Shines

Brunch with the girls. First dates in coffee shops. Baby showers (seriously, it photographs well and won't compete with the cake). Job interviews in creative fields where you want to smell professional but not corporate. Church services if you're into that.

Where it doesn't work: nightclubs, evening dates at dark restaurants, anywhere you want to smell sexy or mysterious. This is not a seduction perfume. It's a "let me introduce you to my parents" perfume.

## FAQ

### Is Calvin Klein Eternity still relevant in 2025?

Relevant is a loaded word. It's not trendy—it doesn't smell like the current market obsession with gourmands or woody ambers. But relevant as in wearable, appropriate, and genuinely pleasant? Absolutely. Think of it as the perfume equivalent of a little black dress. Not exciting, but never wrong.

### How does Eternity compare to the newer Eternity flankers?

Calvin Klein has released multiple versions—Eternity Aqua, Eternity Moment, Eternity Intense. None of them capture what makes the original special. They feel like they're chasing trends rather than setting them. The original Eternity knows what it is and doesn't apologize. The flankers feel like they're trying too hard to be relevant. Stick with the original.

### Will this make me smell old-fashioned or dated?

Honestly? Maybe. But that depends entirely on how you wear it and what your baseline is. If you usually wear Ariana Grande Cloud or Baccarat Rouge 540, yes, Eternity will smell dated to your nose. If you appreciate vintage perfumery and classic structures, it'll smell timeless. Context matters. Confidence matters more.

### Can men wear Eternity for Women?

Fragrance is genderless if you're brave enough. That said, Eternity leans feminine with that lily-freesia core. A man could absolutely wear it—the sage and sandalwood provide enough structure—but you'd be making a statement. There's an Eternity for Men that might be a safer bet if you want the brand vibe with more traditional masculine notes.

### What's the best way to make Eternity last longer?

Layer with purpose. Use a neutral or matching body lotion first. Spray on pulse points but also on clothing—Eternity actually performs better on fabric than skin. And here's a trick: spray once on your hairbrush before brushing your hair. The perfume clings to hair beautifully and releases little bursts of scent when you move. Just don't overdo it or you'll smell like a lily bomb exploded in your face.
