  - question: "How often should I wash my curly hair?"
    answer: "Curly hair should typically be washed 1-3 times per week, depending on your scalp type and lifestyle. Over-washing can strip natural oils that curly hair desperately needs. Many people with curly hair benefit from co-washing (conditioner-only washing) between shampoo days to maintain moisture while gently cleansing."
  - question: "What ingredients should I avoid in curly hair products?"
    answer: "Avoid harsh sulfates like sodium lauryl sulfate, drying alcohols (denatured alcohol, alcohol denat), and heavy silicones that build up over time. These ingredients can strip moisture, cause dryness, and weigh down curls. Look for sulfate-free shampoos and water-soluble silicones instead."
  - question: "How can I reduce frizz in my curly hair?"
    answer: "Reduce frizz by maintaining proper moisture levels, using microfiber towels or cotton t-shirts for drying, applying products to soaking wet hair, avoiding touching your hair while it dries, and sleeping on silk or satin pillowcases. Deep conditioning treatments and anti-humidity products also help control frizz."
  - question: "Should I brush my curly hair when it's dry?"
    answer: "Never brush curly hair when dry as this breaks up the curl pattern and causes frizz and breakage. Only detangle when hair is wet and saturated with conditioner, using a wide-tooth comb, detangling brush, or your fingers. Start from the ends and work your way up to minimize damage."

Curly hair is a beautiful gift that requires specialized care and understanding. Unlike straight hair, curly strands have a unique structure with natural bends and curves that create stunning texture but also present specific challenges. The curved shape of curly hair makes it harder for natural oils to travel from the scalp down the hair shaft, resulting in hair that tends to be drier and more prone to frizz. Understanding your curl pattern is the first step to achieving healthy, defined curls. Curl patterns range from loose waves (Type 2) to tight coils (Type 4), with subcategories A, B, and C indicating increasing curl tightness. Each pattern has distinct needs regarding moisture, styling techniques, and product formulations. The key to successful curly hair care lies in embracing the Curly Girl Method principles: avoiding harsh sulfates that strip natural oils, minimizing heat damage, and using gentle cleansing techniques like co-washing. Moisture is paramount for curly hair health. Deep conditioning treatments, leave-in conditioners, and curl creams help maintain hydration and prevent breakage. Proper styling techniques such as plopping, scrunching, and diffusing can enhance your natural curl pattern while minimizing frizz. Product layering is essential - start with lightweight leave-in treatments, add curl-enhancing creams or gels for definition, and finish with oils or serums to seal in moisture. Nighttime protection using silk pillowcases, satin bonnets, or the pineapple method helps preserve curls overnight. Regular trims every 6-8 weeks prevent split ends from traveling up the hair shaft, maintaining curl integrity. Remember that curly hair is naturally more fragile than straight hair, so gentle handling and patience are crucial. Avoid brushing dry curls, instead detangle only when wet with conditioner using wide-tooth combs or your fingers. Weather conditions significantly impact curly hair - humidity can cause frizz while dry climates may require extra moisturizing products. Building a personalized routine based on your specific curl type, porosity level, and lifestyle needs will help you achieve your best curls yet.
