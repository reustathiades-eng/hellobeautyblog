Okay so here's the thing about turning 32—you start noticing these tiny little creases around your eyes that weren't there at 28, and suddenly every skincare brand is screaming "PEPTIDES!" at you. But most peptide serums cost upwards of $80, and I'm not exactly rolling in cash after paying Brooklyn rent. Enter The Ordinary's Buffet, the $20 bottle that's been sitting in my Sephora cart for approximately six months because I kept second-guessing whether something this cheap could actually work.

Spoiler: it kind of does? But not in the way I expected.

I bought this in January when my skin was doing that weird winter thing where it's simultaneously dehydrated and breaking out. My forehead had these expression lines that stayed put even when my face was totally relaxed, and the skin around my mouth just looked... tired. I'd been using retinol three nights a week but wanted something gentler for off-days that would still address the aging thing without making me peel like a snake.

## What's Actually In This $20 Bottle

### The Peptide Cocktail Nobody Explains Properly

The Ordinary calls this "Buffet" because it's literally an all-you-can-eat situation of peptides. There are four different peptide complexes in here, which sounds impressive until you realize that means you're getting lower concentrations of each one compared to a single-peptide serum. It's like ordering the sampler platter instead of a full entrée—you get to try everything, but none of it's a huge portion.

The star players are two types of Matrixyl (3000 and Synthe'6), which have actual clinical studies showing they boost collagen synthesis. Then there's SYN-AKE, which brands love to call "synthetic snake venom" because that sounds dramatic and expensive. What it actually does is temporarily relax facial muscles, kind of like baby Botox in a bottle. Does it work as well as actual Botox? Absolutely not. Does it do *something*? After two months, I think yes.

### The Hydration Backup Dancers

Beyond the peptides, Buffet has a multi-weight hyaluronic acid complex, which means different molecule sizes that penetrate different skin layers. This is why the serum feels hydrating immediately—the smaller HA molecules sink in fast while the bigger ones sit on top and draw moisture to the surface.

There's also Tasmanian pepperberry (cute but mostly there to calm any potential irritation) and some amino acids that act as skin-identical ingredients. Nothing groundbreaking, but nothing offensive either. The formula is fragrance-free, which I appreciate because my skin gets pissy about added scents.

### What's NOT In Here (And Why That Matters)

No vitamin C. No retinol. No acids. This is purely a hydrating peptide serum, which means you can layer it with basically anything without worrying about interactions. That's actually Buffet's biggest strength—it plays well with others. I've used it under tretinoin, mixed with vitamin C, layered with niacinamide, and never had a problem.

## The Texture Test: How It Actually Feels On Skin

### First Impression Out of the Bottle

The consistency is thinner than I expected—definitely more liquid than gel. It's slightly viscous, like water that's been thickened just a touch, and it has this faintly slippery feel when you rub it between your fingers. No scent whatsoever, which is either boring or perfect depending on how you feel about fragrance-free skincare.

The dropper dispenses way too much product if you're not careful. Three drops is plenty for my whole face and neck, but the first time I used it, I squeezed out like six drops and my face felt like a slip-n-slide for ten minutes.

### The Absorption Reality Check

Here's where Buffet gets a little annoying: it doesn't absorb quickly. The brand says it's "water-based" but it definitely leaves this slightly tacky, film-like finish that takes a solid 60-90 seconds to fully sink in. Not sticky exactly, more like... softly grippy? If you try to apply moisturizer or sunscreen on top before it's done absorbing, everything pills up into these little balls of product. Very frustrating when you're rushing to get out the door.

I started applying it, then going to brush my teeth or make coffee, and by the time I came back to my vanity, it had absorbed enough to layer other stuff on top. Morning routine people who need to be done in five minutes, this might drive you nuts.

## My 60-Day Results (The Good, The Meh, and The Unexpected)

### Week 1-2: Just Hydration, Nothing Special

The first two weeks, Buffet basically functioned as a hydrating serum and that's it. My skin felt plumper and more moisturized, especially in the morning. Those dry patches around my nose smoothed out faster. But anti-aging results? Couldn't see any yet, which is totally normal because peptides take time to stimulate actual changes in your skin.

I did notice my makeup sat better on top of this than on bare skin or just moisturizer alone. That slight tackiness actually helped foundation grip without looking cakey.

### Week 3-4: When Things Got Interesting

Around the three-week mark, my boyfriend asked if I'd done something different to my skin because it looked "smoother." I checked in harsh bathroom lighting and yeah, those little textural bumps on my forehead had definitely calmed down. The skin under my eyes looked less crepey—still wrinkled when I smiled, but the resting state was smoother.

The expression lines on my forehead were maybe 20% less noticeable? They hadn't disappeared (this isn't magic), but they weren't as deep when I made faces, and they seemed to bounce back faster to smooth when I relaxed my face.

### Week 5-8: Plateau or Subtle Maintenance?

After the one-month mark, I didn't see dramatic additional changes. My skin maintained that smoother, more hydrated look, but I wasn't waking up every week thinking "wow, even better!" It had reached a plateau of gentle improvement.

The thing is, I was also using tretinoin twice a week during this time, so who knows how much of my results were from Buffet versus the retinoid. That's the reality of using multiple actives—it's hard to isolate what's doing what. But I will say that on nights when I skipped tret and just used Buffet, my skin felt calmer and less reactive the next morning.

## Who Should Actually Buy This (And Who Shouldn't Bother)

### Perfect For Skincare Beginners Dipping Into Anti-Aging

If you're in your late 20s or early 30s and just starting to think about preventative aging, Buffet is honestly perfect. It's gentle, affordable, and gives you exposure to multiple peptide types without overwhelming your skin or your wallet. This was exactly where I was at, and it felt like the right entry point.

It's also great for people with sensitive skin who can't tolerate retinol or acids. My friend with rosacea has been using this for months with zero irritation, and she reacts to literally everything.

### Skip It If You Want Dramatic Results Fast

Here's the honest truth: if you have deep wrinkles or significant sun damage, Buffet alone isn't going to cut it. Peptides are maintenance players, not transformation heroes. You'd be better off investing in tretinoin, a strong retinol, or professional treatments and using Buffet as a supporting player, not the star.

Also, if your main concern is acne, hyperpigmentation, or texture, this won't address those issues. It's purely hydrating and anti-aging focused. I made the mistake of recommending it to my sister who has PIE scarring, and she was like "this did literally nothing for my marks" because yeah, it's not designed for that.

### The Oily Skin Question

I have combination skin that leans dry in winter, but I tested this during summer when my T-zone gets oily. That tacky finish was annoying under makeup when it was humid—it made my face feel like a grease slick by noon. If you're very oily, you might find this too much for daytime. Nighttime would probably be fine though.

## How It Stacks Up Against The Competition

### Buffet vs. The Inkey List Collagen Peptide Serum

The Inkey List's version is also around $15 and has Matrixyl 3000, but it skips the other peptides that Buffet includes. I tried both (because I'm obsessed), and Buffet felt more hydrating and comprehensive. The Inkey List one absorbed faster though, so if you hate waiting for products to sink in, that might be your pick.

### Buffet vs. Paula's Choice Peptide Booster

Paula's Choice is $52 for 20ml versus $17 for 30ml of Buffet (prices vary by retailer). The PC version has eight peptides and feels more elegant—it absorbs faster and layers better. After trying both, I genuinely think Paula's Choice is worth the extra money if you can swing it. The texture alone is so much better. But if you're on a budget, Buffet gets you 70% of the way there for a third of the price.

### Buffet vs. The Ordinary Matrixyl 10% + HA

This is TO's other peptide serum, and honestly? I prefer Buffet. Matrixyl 10% is more targeted (just one peptide at higher concentration) but feels stickier and less elegant. Buffet's multi-peptide approach means you're hitting different pathways for collagen production, which theoretically is more effective. Though if you want to be a nerd about it, you could rotate between the two.

## The Real Talk On Layering and Routine Placement

### Where It Fits In My Morning Lineup

After cleansing and toning, I apply three drops of Buffet to damp skin (this helps it spread better and absorb faster). Wait 60-90 seconds. Then I go in with niacinamide serum, wait another 30 seconds, then moisturizer, then sunscreen. 

The key is patience. If you rush this, everything pills. I learned this the hard way when I was late for work and tried to slap on SPF 30 seconds after applying Buffet—it rolled up into disgusting little balls all over my face and I had to start over.

### Nighttime Layering With Actives

On retinoid nights: Cleanse, Buffet on damp skin, wait 10 minutes, then tretinoin on totally dry skin, then a heavier moisturizer to buffer. The Buffet actually helps keep my skin hydrated enough that I don't peel as much from tret.

On non-retinoid nights: Cleanse, Buffet, vitamin C or azelaic acid, then a peptide moisturizer. Yeah, I'm doubling up on peptides, sue me. My skin seems to like it.

### What NOT to Mix It With

Honestly, I haven't found anything that conflicts with Buffet. I've used it with every active in my arsenal—vitamin C, niacinamide, AHAs, BHAs, retinol—and never had a reaction. That's the beauty of peptides; they're pretty universally compatible. The only issue is textural (the pilling thing), not chemical.

## The Price-Per-Use Math That Actually Matters

At $17 for 30ml (or $29 for the bigger 60ml bottle if you find it on The Ordinary's website), you're getting about 150 uses from the standard size if you use three drops per application. That's 11 cents per use. Even if you apply it twice daily, the bottle lasts about two and a half months.

Compare that to Sunday Riley's Luna Oil at $55 for 15ml (which isn't even peptides, I'm just talking price per ml), and you're paying literally five times less for Buffet. Now, Luna is a completely different product with different benefits, but in terms of getting peptides into your skin without selling a kidney, Buffet wins.

The value is honestly insane. This is one of those rare cases where the cheap option is actually good enough that I don't feel like I'm compromising.

## What I Wish The Ordinary Would Fix

The dropper. Oh my god, the dropper. It dispenses too much, and the bottle design means product gets all over the outside of the dropper tube, which feels wasteful. I've started storing it upright in a cup so I don't get serum all over my shelf.

Also, the tackiness. I know some people don't notice this or don't mind it, but for me, it's the one thing keeping Buffet from being a total slam dunk. If they could reformulate to absorb just 30 seconds faster without changing the peptide concentrations, it would be perfect.

And this is petty, but the name "Buffet" tells you nothing about what the product does. New people to The Ordinary are always confused about what this is for. "Peptide Complex Serum" would be way more helpful, but I guess "Buffet" sounds cuter for social media.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use Buffet with retinol or tretinoin?**

Yes, absolutely. I use mine with tretinoin twice a week and have had zero issues. Apply Buffet first on clean skin, wait about 10 minutes for it to fully absorb, then apply your retinoid on totally dry skin. The peptides won't interfere with retinol's effectiveness, and the hydration from Buffet can actually help buffer some of the irritation.

**How long does one bottle actually last?**

Using three drops twice a day, the 30ml bottle lasted me about 10 weeks. If you only use it once daily (which is fine for maintenance), you'd get closer to five months out of it. The 60ml bottle would last roughly double that. Honestly, one of the best values in skincare.

**Will this help with deep wrinkles or is it just preventative?**

Real talk: Buffet is better for prevention and very early fine lines. If you have deep-set wrinkles, you'll see some softening but not dramatic erasure. Peptides work by stimulating your skin to produce more collagen and elastin over time, but they can't undo years of sun damage on their own. For deep wrinkles, you'd want prescription retinoids, professional treatments like microneedling, or injectables. Use Buffet as a supporting player, not the star.

**Can I use this if I have oily or acne-prone skin?**

You can, but be aware of the slightly tacky texture. If you're very oily, this might feel like too much during the day, especially under makeup. I'd recommend using it at night only if you're super oily. It's not comedogenic and won't cause breakouts, but the hydration level might be more than oily skin needs in humid weather.

**Is Buffet better than The Ordinary's Matrixyl 10% serum?**

I personally prefer Buffet because you're getting multiple peptides instead of just one type, which means you're addressing collagen production through different pathways. Matrixyl 10% has a higher concentration of that specific peptide, but it's stickier and less elegant. If you want targeted treatment, go Matrixyl. If you want comprehensive, go Buffet. Or do what I did and buy both because they're cheap enough to experiment.
