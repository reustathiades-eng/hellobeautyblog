Okay so I've had a complicated relationship with Drunk Elephant. For years I wrote them off as overpriced Instagram bait with cute packaging and nothing behind it. Then my combination skin hit a wall last winter—dry patches around my nose, but still oily by 2pm, looking just... tired. My derm casually mentioned peptides, and I remembered this jar sitting in my cabinet from a press event I'd never actually opened.

That was six weeks ago. I'm annoyed to report that I was wrong about Protini.

## What Actually Happens When You Put Peptides On Your Face

Here's the thing about peptides that nobody explains properly: they're basically tiny protein fragments that tell your skin cells to do stuff. Signal peptides (which is what Protini uses) specifically tell fibroblasts to make more collagen. It's not instant, it's not dramatic, but it's real.

### The Signal Peptide Story in This Formula

Drunk Elephant loads this with multiple peptide types—signal peptides, carrier peptides, and neurotransmitter peptides. I know that sounds like marketing speak, but each type actually has a different job. Signal peptides trigger collagen production. Carrier peptides deliver trace minerals that help stabilize collagen. Neurotransmitter peptides (in theory) help with expression lines, though that's always been harder for me to measure.

The concentration isn't listed, which drives me crazy about clean beauty brands. They'll tell you what's NOT in there all day but get cagey about percentages. Based on where peptides sit in the ingredient list (high up, after glycerin), I'd guess we're looking at a solid 5-8% peptide complex. That's enough to actually do something.

### The Pygmy Waterlily Thing Everyone Ignores

Buried in the formula is pygmy waterlily stem cell extract, and honestly? I think this is doing more heavy lifting than the marketing suggests. Stem cell extracts are packed with growth factors and antioxidants. In my first two weeks using this, I noticed my skin just looked... calmer. Less reactive. That waterlily extract is probably why.

## The Texture Situation (And Why It Matters More Than You Think)

When I first squeezed this out, I was genuinely confused. It comes out looking like a rich cream—off-white, substantial, the kind of texture that makes you think "okay this is definitely going to sit on my skin." Then you touch it and it completely transforms.

### How It Feels Going On

The second your fingers make contact, it melts into this bouncy gel consistency. Not watery, but definitely not creamy anymore. It's like those color-changing toys from the 90s but for skincare. I use about a pea-and-a-half size for my whole face, and it spreads shockingly well.

Application takes maybe 15 seconds to work it in fully. There's this brief moment where your skin looks slightly shiny, and then it just... absorbs. Completely. No film, no tackiness, no waiting around before you can apply makeup. By the time I've finished my coffee, my skin feels plump but not greasy.

### The Morning vs. Night Experience

I use this both AM and PM, and it behaves differently depending on what's underneath. In the morning after vitamin C serum, it sinks in fast and plays well under sunscreen. At night over retinol (I use Medik8 Crystal Retinal), it feels more occlusive and protective. My skin is noticeably softer when I wake up.

The one layering issue I had: it doesn't love going over heavy face oils. I tried it after Sunday Riley's Luna Oil once and it just pilled up into little balls. Not cute. If you're an oil user, either skip the oil or use this before it, not after.

## Week One Through Week Six: The Actual Timeline

I'm going to be really specific here because "results may vary" is useless information.

### Week 1: The Hydration Hit

First three days, nothing dramatic. My skin felt moisturized, which is literally the baseline job of a moisturizer, so congrats I guess? But by day five, I noticed my makeup was sitting better. Foundation wasn't clinging to dry patches around my nose anymore. That's hydration working—this has multiple molecular weights of hyaluronic acid plus amino acids that pull water into your skin.

### Weeks 2-3: When Texture Started Shifting

Around the two-week mark, my boyfriend asked if I was "doing something different" with my skin. I wasn't—same routine, just swapped in Protini. My pores looked smaller, particularly on my cheeks. Not gone (let's be realistic), but definitely less noticeable. The pygmy waterlily and peptides were probably starting to do their thing here.

I also went through a horrible stress breakout during week three. Here's what I noticed: the breakouts happened (peptides aren't going to stop hormonal acne), but my skin bounced back faster. Less post-inflammatory redness, less lingering rough texture after spots healed. That's the amino acid and protein support at work.

### Week 4-6: The Plumping Everyone Talks About

By week four, okay, I saw it. The "Protini plump" is real. My nasolabial folds looked softer. Not erased—I'm not delusional—but definitely less pronounced. The fine lines around my eyes that get worse in winter were less visible. My skin just looked... fuller. Bouncier. Like I'd been sleeping eight hours a night instead of my usual chaotic six.

Week six is where I am now, and my skin texture is legitimately better than it was in November. Smoother, more even, better at reflecting light. I look more awake without concealer, which is honestly the biggest compliment I can give a moisturizer.

## The Clean Beauty Formula Breakdown (What's Actually In This Thing)

Drunk Elephant makes a huge deal about their "Suspicious 6" list of ingredients they won't use. No essential oils, no fragrance, no silicones, no SLS, no chemical sunscreens. Cool. But what's actually IN here that's working?

### Beyond the Peptides: The Supporting Cast

Soybean folic acid ferment is in here, and it's one of those ingredients that doesn't get enough credit. It's a byproduct of fermentation that's rich in amino acids and brightening compounds. I think this is partly why my skin looks more radiant—not glowy like I'm covered in highlighter, but that healthy skin radiance that comes from good cell turnover.

Sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer is the fancy version of hyaluronic acid that can penetrate deeper and holds even more water. My skin definitely feels more hydrated from the inside, not just surface-level moisture.

### What's NOT In Here (And Why I Actually Care)

No silicones means no dimethicone, which is usually what gives moisturizers that silky, pore-blurring effect. Protini achieves smoothness through plant-derived esters instead. It works, but it's different—less immediate blur, more actual texture improvement over time.

No fragrance is huge for me. My skin isn't super sensitive, but fragranced products make my eyes water if I apply them too close. This has zero scent. Literally smells like nothing, maybe slightly fatty from the plant butters, but that's it.

## Who Should Actually Buy This (And Who Shouldn't)

Let's talk money. $68 for 50ml. That's $1.36 per milliliter, which puts it firmly in premium territory but not quite luxury. For context, La Mer is $4.20/ml, so Protini is expensive but not insane.

### If You're Combination Like Me

This is basically perfect. It's hydrating enough for dry areas but doesn't make my T-zone greasy. I still get slightly oily by afternoon, but way less than with richer creams. It's that gel-cream texture sweet spot—substantial enough to moisturize, light enough not to suffocate.

### If You Have Oily Skin

You might find this too much, honestly. The peptide complex includes some richer ingredients, and while it's not heavy, it's not a gel moisturizer either. Try the Inkey List's Peptide Moisturizer first at $14.99 if you want to test peptides on oily skin without the investment.

### If You're Dry or Mature

Here's where I have mixed feelings. This is great for mild to moderate dryness, especially if you layer serums underneath. But if you're truly dry or dealing with compromised barrier (think flaking, tightness, sensitivity), you'll need something richer at night. Layer this in the morning, use Protini + a facial oil at night, or save your money and get Stratia Liquid Gold for better barrier repair at half the price.

### If You're Acne-Prone

The formula is non-comedogenic, but it does contain marula oil near the end of the ingredient list. Most people are fine with it, but if you're someone who breaks out from even light oils, patch test first. I'm mildly acne-prone and had zero issues, but everyone's different.

ICE is $65 for 50ml, so basically the same price point. ICE is better for barrier repair—those ceramides are clutch if your skin is sensitized or dehydrated. But Protini is better for anti-aging concerns. The peptides in Protini are more focused on collagen production and firmness, while ICE is about hydration and protection. I actually alternate between them depending on what my skin needs.

## FAQ: The Questions Everyone Actually Asks

### Can you use Protini with retinol or tretinoin?

Yes, absolutely. I use it over Medik8's retinal product without issues. The peptides actually complement retinoids nicely—retinoids stimulate collagen production through cell turnover, peptides signal your skin to make more collagen directly. Just make sure your retinoid is fully absorbed (wait 10-15 minutes) before applying Protini.

### Does Protini break you out?

It didn't break me out, and I'm moderately acne-prone. The formula is non-comedogenic and pretty clean. That said, it contains marula oil, and some people react to that. If you're very acne-prone or sensitive to oils, patch test first. The r/SkincareAddiction consensus seems to be that most people tolerate it fine.

### Is Protini better than Lala Retro (the other Drunk Elephant moisturizer)?

Different purposes. Lala Retro is richer, more focused on barrier repair with ceramides and fatty acids. It's better if you're dry or dealing with compromised skin. Protini is more focused on anti-aging and plumping with peptides. If you're under 30 with normal/combo skin, Protini. If you're dry, sensitive, or dealing with barrier issues, Lala Retro.

### Can you use Protini if you're pregnant or breastfeeding?

Yes. Peptides are safe during pregnancy and breastfeeding. This doesn't contain retinoids, no high-dose vitamin C that might be irritating, no essential oils. It's one of the safer anti-aging options for pregnancy. But obviously check with your doctor about your specific situation.

### Does it work on mature skin or is this just for younger people?

My mom is 58 and I gave her a sample. She found it too light for her needs at night but loved it for daytime under makeup. For mature skin, I'd say use this in the morning and something richer at night (Lala Retro, or layer this with a facial oil). The peptides absolutely work on mature skin—if anything, that's when you need them most—but you might need additional moisture support.
