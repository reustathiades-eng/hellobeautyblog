Okay so my T-zone looked like an oil slick by noon every single day, and my pores were so visible I could practically see them from across the room in my Zoom meetings. I'd tried the expensive stuff—Sunday Riley, Drunk Elephant, the whole influencer-approved lineup—and still ended up blotting my nose five times before lunch.

Then I grabbed The Ordinary's Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1% because it was literally $5.90 and I figured if it didn't work, I'd spent more on my morning latte. That was two months ago. Here's what nobody tells you about this cult-favorite serum.

## The Dropper Bottle Reality: First Impressions That Made Me Skeptical

The packaging is clinical. Like, aggressively boring clinical. Clear glass bottle, white dropper cap, ingredients list that looks like it belongs in a chemistry textbook. Zero aesthetic appeal. If you're into beautiful bathroom shelf moments, this isn't it.

First squeeze of the dropper and I got way too much product. The consistency is super thin—almost watery but slightly more viscous, like somewhere between water and aloe vera gel. It's completely clear and has zero scent. And I mean ZERO. Which I actually loved because my combination skin does not play nice with fragrance.

Here's where it got weird: I dropped about four drops onto my palm (which was too much, learned that the hard way), and it felt slippery between my fingers. Not silky-slippery like a nice hyaluronic acid serum. More like... slightly sticky? Like your fingers want to stick together just a tiny bit when you rub them.

## Week One: The Pilling Situation Nobody Warned Me About

I applied it after cleansing and toning, which is exactly where treatment serums should go. Pressed it into my skin and waited for it to absorb.

And waited.

And waited.

It took a solid 60-90 seconds to fully sink in, which felt like forever in skincare time. During that window, if I touched my face or tried to layer my moisturizer too quickly, it would pill. You know that thing where product starts balling up into little rolled pieces on your skin? Yeah. That happened three mornings in a row before I figured out the timing.

The trick: pat it in gently, give it two full minutes, then move on with your routine. Also, you need way less than you think. Three drops max for your entire face.

My skin felt slightly tight after application. Not dry-tight, more like... matte-tight? My usual morning shine was definitely reduced, which was promising. But I didn't see any visible pore changes in week one. My hormonal chin acne that was brewing didn't magically disappear either.

## What 10% Niacinamide Actually Does to Sebum Production

Let's talk science for a second without making this a lecture. Niacinamide is vitamin B3, and at 10% concentration, it's working overtime to regulate how much oil your sebaceous glands pump out. It literally tells your skin "hey, we don't need that much oil, chill out."

The zinc PCA is the wingman here—it's an amino acid derivative that also controls oil and has antimicrobial properties. Together, they're basically the bouncer team for your pores.

But here's the thing about 10% niacinamide: it's a HIGH dose. Most serums use 2-5%. The Ordinary went aggressive with this formula, which is great for oily skin like mine, but can be irritating if you have sensitive or dry skin. More isn't always better in skincare, and I actually think this concentration is overkill for some people.

### The Zinc Addition Nobody Talks About Enough

Everyone fixates on the niacinamide percentage, but that 1% zinc is doing serious work. Zinc regulates enzyme activity that affects oil production. It's also anti-inflammatory, which is why this serum can help with active breakouts.

I noticed my angry red hormonal spots looked less inflamed by day five. They didn't disappear (niacinamide isn't magic), but they went from "everyone is staring at this volcano on my chin" to "I can cover this with concealer and forget about it."

## Week Four: When I Finally Saw the Pore Difference

Around the three-week mark, my boyfriend asked if I was wearing makeup when I wasn't. That's when I looked closer in my magnifying mirror.

My nose pores—which are genetically huge and always will be, thanks Mom—looked smaller. Not closed (that's impossible, pores don't have muscles), but less noticeable. The sebaceous filaments that used to be dark and obvious were lighter. My skin texture around my nose and cheeks looked smoother.

The oil control was the real winner though. I went from blotting 4-5 times a day to maybe once in the afternoon. My makeup stayed on better. That dewy-but-actually-just-oily situation I'd battle by 2 PM was mostly gone.

But my skin also felt slightly dehydrated. This is the trade-off with oil control—if you're not careful with your moisturizer game, niacinamide at this strength can tip you into that tight, uncomfortable feeling. I had to upgrade to a more occlusive moisturizer to compensate.

### The Texture Evolution: Two Months In

By week eight, my skin texture was genuinely better. Those rough bumps I'd get on my forehead—not quite acne, not quite closed comedones, just annoying texture—were smoothed out significantly.

My hyperpigmentation from old acne scars faded slightly. Niacinamide inhibits melanosome transfer, which is a fancy way of saying it prevents pigment from darkening your skin. I saw maybe 20-30% improvement in some of my darker spots. Not dramatic, but noticeable in direct sunlight.

## The Morning Versus Evening Application Experiment

I tried this both ways to see what worked better. Morning application made sense because it controlled oil throughout the day. My makeup went on smoother, and I looked less shiny in photos.

Evening application let it work overnight without having to layer makeup on top. No pilling issues. But I woke up with slightly drier skin.

My sweet spot: morning application on my T-zone and any active breakout areas, then a different, more hydrating serum on my cheeks at night. You don't have to use this all over your face if you have combination skin. Spot treatment approach works great.

## Who Should Absolutely Skip This Serum

If you have dry skin, this might be too aggressive. The zinc can be drying, and 10% niacinamide doesn't play nice with already-compromised moisture barriers.

If you're already using tretinoin or strong retinoids, proceed with caution. I learned this the hard way when I tried to use this the same night as my 0.05% tretinoin and woke up with angry, flaking skin. Niacinamide can theoretically work with retinoids, but this high concentration was too much for my skin to handle together. Pick one for evening, use the other in the morning.

If you have rosacea or very sensitive skin, patch test first. That tingling some people feel? It's not purging, it's irritation. The Tasmanian pepperberry is supposed to calm things down, but some people still react.

### The Sensitive Skin Alternative

If this formula is too strong for you, try Paula's Choice 10% Niacinamide Booster instead. It's more expensive ($44 versus $6), but the formula is gentler and doesn't have the zinc, so it's less likely to cause dryness. Or go for a lower concentration like Glossier's Super Pure (5% niacinamide) if you want to ease into it.

## How This Actually Stacks Up Against Competitors

I've used Paula's Choice Niacinamide Booster, and honestly, the results were similar but the texture was better—no pilling, faster absorption. But it's 7x the price. For my oily, resilient skin, The Ordinary version works just fine once you figure out the application technique.

Compared to Naturium's Niacinamide Serum 12% Plus Zinc 2%, The Ordinary is less aggressive. Yes, seriously, even though Naturium has higher percentages, the formula feels gentler on my skin. The Ordinary's texture is more problematic, though.

The Inkey List has a Niacinamide serum that's basically The Ordinary's formula but with a slightly better texture. If the pilling drives you crazy, try that one instead. Same price point, near-identical ingredient list, less fussy application.

## The Real Cost Per Use Breakdown

At $5.90 for 30ml, and using 3 drops twice daily, this bottle lasts about 2-3 months. That's roughly $2-3 per month. I spend more on coffee in a single day.

Even if you experience the pilling, even if the texture isn't luxurious, even if you have to wait 90 seconds for absorption—at this price point, it's hard to complain. I've spent $68 on serums that did less for my pores.

The value is genuinely absurd. This is one of the few products where "affordable" doesn't mean "compromised results."

## My Final Verdict After 60 Days of Consistent Use

This serum did what it promised: controlled my oil production, minimized the appearance of my pores, and helped fade some hyperpigmentation. My skin looks more refined, my makeup sits better, and I'm not frantically blotting by noon.

But it's not perfect. The texture is finicky, the application requires patience, and it can be drying if you're not careful with your moisturizer. It's also not going to work miracles on deep acne scars or closed comedones—it's a maintenance product, not a transformation product.

For oily and combination skin types dealing with enlarged pores and excess sebum, this is absolutely worth trying. For $6, the risk is minimal and the potential payoff is significant. Just manage your expectations, give it a full month to see results, and be prepared to adjust your routine around its quirks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use niacinamide with vitamin C in the same routine?**

Yes, despite old internet myths saying they can't be combined. I use my vitamin C serum in the morning, wait for it to absorb, then apply this niacinamide serum with zero issues. The pH incompatibility thing was debunked years ago, but the myth persists.

**Why does this serum feel sticky and pill on my skin?**

The formula has a high concentration of water-soluble actives without much in the way of elegant texture agents (because that would increase the cost). Let it absorb fully before layering other products, use less product than you think you need, and pat rather than rub. If it's still pilling, check your other products—silicone-heavy primers and niacinamide don't always play nice.

**How long until I see results for pore size?**

I saw subtle changes around week three, more obvious results by week five. Pore size reduction isn't dramatic because pores don't actually shrink—they just appear smaller when they're not clogged with sebum and dead skin. If you're not seeing anything by week six, this might not be the right formula for your skin concerns.

**Can I use this if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?**

Niacinamide is generally considered safe during pregnancy and breastfeeding, but always check with your healthcare provider. I'm not a doctor, and everyone's situation is different.

**Does this help with acne scarring or just active acne?**

It helps with both, but in different ways. For active acne, the zinc and niacinamide reduce inflammation and regulate oil. For post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (those dark spots after acne heals), niacinamide gradually fades discoloration over several weeks. For actual textured scars (ice pick, boxcar, rolling scars), you'll need stronger interventions like retinoids, chemical peels, or professional treatments. This is a supporting player, not the star of that show.
