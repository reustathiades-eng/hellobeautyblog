Okay so I have a confession: I bought TLC Framboos because of the bottle. That marbled raspberry pink design sitting on my bathroom shelf just makes me feel like I have my life together, even when I definitely don't. But then I saw the $90 price tag and panicked—this gel better change my entire face or I'm returning it.

Six weeks later, my forehead is smoother than it's been since 2019, my pores around my nose have actually shrunk (I didn't think that was possible without Photoshop), and I've gone through exactly half the bottle. Which means I'm doing the mental math on whether I can justify repurchasing, because here's the thing: this serum is expensive, it's strong, and it absolutely works—but not without some trial and error.

## What's Actually Inside This $90 Raspberry Bottle

### The 12% AHA Blend That Does the Heavy Lifting

Let me break down what "12% AHA blend" actually means without getting too science-y. Drunk Elephant combined glycolic acid (the smallest molecule that penetrates deepest), lactic acid (gentler, more hydrating), tartaric acid (brightening), and citric acid (pH adjusting) into one overnight exfoliating cocktail. The glycolic is the star here—it's literally dissolving the bonds between dead skin cells so they slough off while you sleep.

I have combination skin that gets oily in my T-zone but dry around my cheeks during winter. The first week, I could feel this working. Like, that tight, slightly tingly sensation that makes you wonder if you're exfoliating or just irritating your face. For me, it was the former, but I'm getting ahead of myself.

### The 2% Salicylic Acid Addition Nobody Expected

Here's where Framboos gets interesting: it's not just an AHA serum. They threw in 2% salicylic acid (a BHA) which is oil-soluble, meaning it dives into your pores instead of just working on the surface. This combo is why the serum works so well on texture AND congestion. If you've got those annoying closed comedones on your forehead or chin (I had a whole colony of them), the salicylic acid targets those while the AHAs handle the dull, rough surface.

But also—and this is crucial—this is why you can't use this serum carelessly. Combining AHAs and BHAs is powerful. Too powerful if you're also using retinol, vitamin C, or any other actives without a strategy.

### The Supporting Cast: Peptides and Horse Chestnut

Drunk Elephant added matrixyl 3000 (a peptide that supposedly helps with fine lines) and horse chestnut extract for antioxidants. Honestly? I can't tell you if these are doing much compared to the acids. The acids are so strong that everything else feels like a bonus feature I didn't pay attention to. It's like buying a car for the heated seats but the engine is what you actually notice.

## My 6-Week Experience: The Good, The Flaky, and The Glowy

### Week 1: Am I Burning My Face Off?

First application: I used 3-4 drops (the bottle has a pump, which I appreciate) on clean, dry skin. The texture is a clear gel that's slightly thicker than water but spreads easily. No scent, which is perfect because my nose is sensitive to fragrance. It absorbed in maybe 20 seconds, leaving my skin feeling smooth but slightly tight.

I woke up the next morning and my skin looked... brighter? But also felt a bit raw. Not irritated exactly, just more sensitive when I washed my face. By day three, I had some flaking around my chin. Nothing dramatic, but definitely noticeable when I tried to apply foundation. I cut back to every other night.

### Week 2-3: The Purge Phase Nobody Warns You About

Here's what Drunk Elephant doesn't plaster on the bottle: you might purge. I got three small pimples on my forehead during week two—areas where I usually get congestion but hadn't broken out in months. I almost quit. But I'd read enough about chemical exfoliation to know that bringing all that gunk to the surface is part of the process.

By week three, those pimples were gone, and the texture on my forehead—those tiny bumps I'd had for literally years—started smoothing out. This is when I became a believer. I bumped back up to nightly use, but alternated with my hydrating serum on nights my skin felt cranky.

### Week 4-6: The "Wait, Is This Actually Working?" Phase

Around week four, my boyfriend asked if I'd gotten a facial. I hadn't. My skin just looked smoother, more even-toned, and legitimately glowy in natural light. The pores around my nose (my biggest insecurity) appeared smaller—not gone, because pores don't disappear, but definitely less noticeable.

My makeup went on smoother. I stopped needing as much concealer. The fine lines around my eyes didn't budge (this isn't a retinol), but the overall texture of my skin improved so much that those lines bothered me less. If that makes sense.

## The Application Strategy That Actually Works

### Building Tolerance Without Destroying Your Moisture Barrier

Start slow. I cannot stress this enough. Even if you've used acids before, 12% AHA plus 2% BHA is no joke. My strategy that worked:
- Week 1-2: Every other night, on clean dry skin
- Week 3: Every night, but skip if skin feels sensitive
- Week 4+: Nightly, with occasional breaks if traveling or stressed (stress makes my skin more reactive)

Always wait until your skin is completely dry before applying. Damp skin = increased penetration = higher risk of irritation. I learned this the hard way when I got impatient one night and applied it 30 seconds after washing my face. Woke up with red patches on my cheeks.

### What to Layer It With (And What to Absolutely Avoid)

After Framboos absorbs, wait 3-5 minutes, then follow with a hydrating serum or moisturizer. I use Drunk Elephant's Protini (yes, I'm that person who bought into the whole line) or just CeraVe Moisturizing Cream when I'm trying to save money.

Do NOT use this with:
- Retinol (on the same night—alternate them)
- Vitamin C (too much exfoliation + vitamin C = irritation city)
- Other AHAs/BHAs (why would you need more?)
- Physical exfoliants (absolutely not)

I use vitamin C in the morning, Framboos at night, and retinol 2-3x per week on nights I skip Framboos. This rotation keeps my skin happy without overdoing it.

### The Sunscreen Lecture You Already Know But I'm Saying Anyway

AHAs make your skin more sun-sensitive. You MUST wear SPF 30+ every single day. Not negotiable. I use EltaMD UV Clear because it doesn't pill under makeup, but honestly any broad-spectrum SPF works. Just wear it. Even in winter. Even when it's cloudy. Your future self will thank you.

## How It Compares to Other Exfoliating Serums I've Tried

### The Ordinary Glycolic Acid 7% Toning Solution ($9)

I used this for six months before Framboos. It's good, especially for the price, but it's a toner (watery consistency) with 7% glycolic acid only. Results were slower and less dramatic. If you're new to acids or on a budget, start here. But if you've plateaued with The Ordinary like I did, Framboos is the upgrade that actually delivers.

### Paula's Choice 2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant ($32)

This targets pores and breakouts beautifully because it's BHA-only. I still use it as a spot treatment. But it doesn't give the overall resurfacing and glow that Framboos does. They serve different purposes. If I had to choose one, I'd pick Framboos for overall texture and brightness.

### Sunday Riley Good Genes ($85)

The closest competitor. Good Genes uses lactic acid (gentler than glycolic), smells weird (like lemon-scented bandaids), and costs almost the same. I prefer Framboos because the glycolic + salicylic combo works better for my oily T-zone and congestion. But if you have sensitive or dry skin, Good Genes might be the better choice.

## Who Should Buy This (And Who Should Run Away)

### This Serum Is Perfect For You If:

- You have normal to oily skin that can handle strong actives
- Your main concerns are texture, dullness, congestion, or early signs of aging
- You've used acids before and want something more effective
- You're willing to invest in a product and use it consistently for 4+ weeks
- You're religious about sunscreen (seriously, if you skip SPF, don't buy this)

### Skip Framboos If:

- You have sensitive, rosacea-prone, or very dry skin (this will wreck you)
- You're already using prescription retinoids like tretinoin (too much exfoliation)
- You're pregnant or breastfeeding (check with your doctor about strong acids)
- You want instant results (this takes weeks)
- You can't commit to nightly use for at least a month (waste of money otherwise)

I have combination skin that leans oily in summer, and I still had to build tolerance. If you're on the sensitive side, either start with something gentler or use this once or twice a week max.

## The Price Reality Check: Is It Worth $90?

### Breaking Down the Cost Per Use

The bottle is 30ml (1 oz) and lasts about 3 months with nightly use. That's $30/month or about $1 per use. Compared to getting monthly facials ($100-150 each), it's reasonable. Compared to The Ordinary ($9 for a product that lasts 3 months), it's expensive.

Here's my honest take: if you have the budget and your skin can handle it, yes, it's worth it. The results are visible and consistent. But if $90 makes you anxious, try The Ordinary's glycolic acid toner or Paula's Choice BHA first. You might get 70% of the results for 20% of the price.

### When to Buy and When to Wait

I'd splurge on Framboos when:
- You've plateaued with gentler acids
- You have a specific event coming up (wedding, photos) and need fast results
- You're treating yourself and want that luxury experience
- You find it on sale (rare, but Sephora VIB sales happen)

Wait and use something else if:
- You're new to chemical exfoliation
- Your budget is tight this month
- You're dealing with active breakouts or compromised skin barrier
- You haven't nailed down your sunscreen situation yet

No immediate gratification. If you're the type who needs to see results tomorrow, you'll be disappointed. This is a slow-burn product that requires patience and consistency.

The price. I've already ranted about this, but $90 for a serum that lasts 3 months is a lot. I justify it because it works, but I totally understand people who can't or won't spend that much.

## My Final Verdict After 6 Weeks

I'm buying a second bottle. That tells you everything, honestly. My skin looks better, feels smoother, and responds better to makeup. The flaking during week one was annoying, the purge during week two was concerning, but the results from week four onward made it all worth it.

This isn't a gentle, everyone-can-use-it serum. It's strong, requires strategy, and demands that you wear sunscreen like your skin depends on it (because it does). But if you have oily or combination skin, struggle with texture and dullness, and want something that actually delivers visible results, Framboos is one of the best exfoliating serums I've tested.

Rating: 4.5/5. Half a star deducted for price and the adjustment period, but the results earned those 4.5 stars legitimately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use TLC Framboos every night?**

Eventually, yes, but not right away. Start with every other night for at least two weeks. Listen to your skin—if you're getting flaky, red, or irritated, you're overdoing it. I worked up to nightly use by week three, but some people max out at 3-4 times per week, and that's fine. More isn't always better with acids.

**Should I use this in the morning or at night?**

Night only. AHAs increase sun sensitivity, and you're supposed to let this work on your skin for several hours without layering SPF or makeup on top. Plus, your skin repairs itself overnight, so you're maximizing the resurfacing benefits. Save your mornings for vitamin C and sunscreen.

**Can I use TLC Framboos with retinol?**

Not on the same night. Both are powerful exfoliants that increase cell turnover. Using them together is a fast track to irritation and a damaged moisture barrier. I alternate: Framboos on Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday; retinol on Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday. Or some people do Framboos nightly and retinol 2x per week. Find what works for your skin.

**How long does it take to see results?**

I saw brighter skin within a week, but real texture improvement took 3-4 weeks of consistent use. For pore size reduction and that glowy, smooth finish everyone raves about, give it a full six weeks. This isn't an instant-results product. If you're not seeing anything after eight weeks, it might not be the right product for your skin concerns.

**Is TLC Framboos good for acne?**

It depends on your type of acne. For closed comedones, texture bumps, and mild congestion, yes—the combo of glycolic and salicylic acid works beautifully. For cystic or hormonal acne, you need something more targeted (like a retinoid or prescription treatment). I had some purging in week two where congestion came to the surface, but it cleared quickly. If you're having an active breakout, wait until your skin calms down before introducing strong acids.
